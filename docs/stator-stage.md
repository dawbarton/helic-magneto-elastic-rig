# The stator stage

A stepper-driven micrometer that sets the stator's displacement, and with it
the gap between stator and specimen. The gap is a physical parameter of the
experiment rather than a signal: it is set between measurements, held while a
capture runs, and never swept continuously.

This document is the user-facing description of the axis: what the hardware is,
what the firmware guarantees, how to commission and calibrate it, and what it
deliberately does not do. Hardware evidence belongs in `notes.md`, not here.

## Summary of what the firmware guarantees

- Step pulses are generated by a PIO state machine, so their timing does not
  depend on core-0 load, and a starved FIFO stretches one step interval rather
  than losing or duplicating a pulse.
- Nothing about the axis runs on core 1. The real-time tick reads one atomic
  word to log the stator position alongside every sample, and that is the whole
  of its involvement.
- Every commanded move ends with motion in the advance direction, so the
  mechanism is always left in the same contact state.
- Absolute position is meaningful only after homing. Before that, absolute
  moves are refused and the reported position is `NaN`.

## Hardware

### Driver

A [Pololu 2966](https://www.pololu.com/product/2966), which carries the MPS
**MP6500** with potentiometer current control. Relevant characteristics:

- STEP and DIR accept 3.3 V logic directly, and both have internal pull-downs,
  so a Hi-Z microcontroller pin is a defined (inactive) input.
- Minimum STEP pulse width is 1 µs. The firmware emits 8 µs.
- MS1 and MS2 select full, half, 1/4, or 1/8 step, and both are pulled low
  internally. **An unwired carrier is therefore in full step.**
- Motor supply 4.5–35 V, up to 2.5 A per phase set by the potentiometer.

### Connector

The nine-pin connector as supplied:

| Pin | Signal | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GND | |
| 2 | +3.3 V | supply for the opto sensor |
| 3 | unused | see "Wiring changes worth making" |
| 4 | Opto sensor output | |
| 5 | STEP | |
| 6 | GND | |
| 7 | +12 V (approx) | motor supply |
| 8 | unused | see "Wiring changes worth making" |
| 9 | DIR | |

### Pin map

Assigned in `src/board.rs`. These were the free GPIOs on the W5500-EVB-Pico2
once the WIZnet controller (GP16–21, GP25), the AD7609, the AD5064, the laser
UART, and the tick output had taken theirs.

| Signal | GPIO | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| STEP | GP22 | PIO0 state machine 0, side-set |
| DIR | GP26 | output |
| Opto sensor | GP27 | input, pulled up |
| ENABLE (nSLEEP) | GP28 | output |

### Wiring changes worth making

Two changes materially improve the axis, and neither is expensive.

**Microstepping, at no cost in conductors.** Pin 2 already carries 3.3 V to the
driver end. Strapping MS1 and MS2 to that rail there selects 1/8 microstepping
without using either spare conductor. The MS inputs are high impedance with
internal pull-downs, so the load on the opto supply is tens of microamps. This
takes the resolution from 3.175 µm to 0.397 µm per step and, more importantly,
greatly reduces the mechanical transient each step injects into the specimen.

**ENABLE, using spare pin 3.** Without nSLEEP the driver chops current through
the motor continuously, even at rest. On this rig that means a permanently
energised switching source of a few watts bolted to the mechanism, next to a
sense coil whose noise floor is a few hundred microvolts, and a heat source in
the micrometer's own thermal path. Steel at 11 ppm/K over 25 mm of travel is
0.3 µm/K, which is comparable to a single microstep, so this is a measurement
concern and not only a comfort one.

The firmware assumes both changes will be made. Until they are:

- set `STATOR_MICROSTEPS` in `src/config.rs` to `1.0` (its current value), and
- the ENABLE output will simply drive a pin nothing is connected to, which is
  harmless.

Fit an external pull-down on the ENABLE line so that a reset or unpowered
microcontroller leaves the motor de-energised rather than floating.

### Grounding and supply

Pins 1 and 6 are both ground. If they are commoned at the connector, the
chopped motor return current, amps at around 100 kHz, shares a path with the
opto sensor's 3.3 V reference and, through the microcontroller, with the
analogue front end. Bring them back separately to a single star point.

The MP6500 needs its bulk electrolytic close to the carrier. The inductive
spike from hot-plugging a motor supply is a documented way to destroy these
boards, so sequence the supplies rather than hot-plugging.

Set the current-limit potentiometer to give just enough torque plus margin.
This is the axis's only mechanical fuse: with no limit switches beyond the opto
sensor, a crash into the micrometer's hard stop should stall the motor, not
damage the thread or the specimen.

## Resolution, travel, and what is assumed

The micrometer is imperial: one turn of the barrel is 1/40 inch, which is
0.635 mm **exactly**. Nothing in the firmware's millimetre arithmetic is a
rounded conversion.

Assuming a standard 1.8° two-phase motor at 200 full steps per revolution,
coupled directly to the barrel:

| Quantity | Full step | 1/8 microstep |
|---|---|---|
| Per step | 3.175 µm | 0.396875 µm |
| Per revolution | 200 steps | 1600 steps |
| Over 25 mm of travel | 7874 steps | 62 992 steps |

**The motor is not yet identified**, so 200 full steps per revolution is an
assumption to be confirmed at commissioning, as is the absence of any gearing
or belt reduction between motor and barrel. Both are checked by the
steps-per-millimetre procedure below.

### The one constant that can bite

`STATOR_MICROSTEPS` in `src/config.rs` must match the MS1/MS2 strapping. The
error is asymmetric, and the current default is chosen accordingly:

- Setting it **too low** (1 when the hardware is 1/8) makes every move eight
  times too **short**. Harmless.
- Setting it **too high** (8 when the hardware is full step) makes every move
  eight times too **long**, straight into the end stop.

Note that the soft travel limits do **not** protect against this, because they
are converted to steps with the same wrong constant. The protections that do
work are the current-limit potentiometer and the commissioning check below.
The constant therefore defaults to `1.0`, matching an unmodified carrier, and
must be raised to `8.0` only once the strapping is confirmed by inspection.

### Datum repeatability sets the accuracy budget

Previous testing indicates the opto sensor gives about 1/1000 inch, that is
25.4 µm, of repeatability. That is 8 full steps, or 64 microsteps, and it is
the dominant error term. Three consequences:

- **Relative moves are far more precise than absolute ones.** Step counting
  resolves 0.4 µm; the datum is good to 25 µm.
- **Home once per session, then trust the counter.** Re-homing between
  measurements injects 25 µm of jitter that step counting would not have.
- **The lost-step audit has a noise floor of about 64 microsteps.** A re-home
  can detect gross step loss, not a handful of steps.

## Mechanical constraints

These dominate the design and are worth understanding before using the axis.

### The stage is not spring preloaded

A micrometer spindle can push but it cannot pull. With no preload spring, the
stage follows the spindle when advancing and is simply released when
retracting: **after a retract, the stage position is undefined** until a
subsequent advance re-establishes contact.

This is why the unidirectional approach described below is not a refinement to
reduce backlash by a few microns, it is the only thing that makes the axis
deterministic at all. It is also why the firmware tracks a "settled" flag
separately from the step count, and reports position as `NaN` when a move was
aborted part-way through a retract.

**Fitting a preload spring would be a real improvement**, and would reduce the
retract-then-advance sequence from a necessity to an optimisation. If the
magnetic load on the stator reverses sign anywhere over the travel, a spring
that is adequate at one end may not be at the other, so size it against the
worst case.

### Couple to the thimble, not the ratchet

Many micrometers have a friction or ratchet cap that slips at a set torque. A
motor coupled to the ratchet will turn happily while the spindle does not.
Couple to the thimble body or the spindle directly, and release the locking
clamp.

### Magnetic coupling

A permanent-magnet stepper is being bolted to a magneto-elastic rig with a
sense coil. Rotor rotation may induce voltage in the coil directly, and the
rig's own excitation field may exert torque on the rotor. Use a non-magnetic
mount and coupling, and give the motor as much standoff from the specimen as
the geometry allows. This is the coupling mechanism most likely to be missed,
and the noise characterisation below is partly there to find it.

## Using the axis

### Parameters

Writable parameters are discovered by name, like every other parameter on this
rig. They are `f32` and are validated for range on write; validation that
depends on axis state, such as refusing an absolute move before homing, happens
in the stepper task and is reported through `stator_faults` rather than as a
write error.

| Parameter | Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `rig_stator_target` | mm | absolute target, as a micrometer reading. Requires homing |
| `rig_stator_jog` | mm | relative move. Permitted before homing |
| `rig_stator_home` | — | write any non-zero value to run the homing sequence |
| `rig_stator_stop` | — | write any non-zero value to abort the move in progress |
| `rig_stator_rate` | mm/s | traverse rate |
| `rig_stator_backlash` | mm | overshoot used by the unidirectional approach |
| `rig_stator_datum` | mm | the micrometer reading at the opto datum |
| `rig_stator_hold` | — | 1 holds current between moves, 0 de-energises |

Read-only telemetry:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `stator_position_mm` | current position as a micrometer reading, `NaN` if unknown |
| `stator_steps` | signed microsteps from the datum |
| `stator_state` | 0 idle, 1 moving, 2 homing, 3 not homed, 4 faulted |
| `stator_homed` | 1 once a homing sequence has completed |
| `stator_home_error` | microsteps between predicted and actual datum at the last re-home |
| `stator_moves` | completed moves, for wear and audit |
| `stator_faults` | rejected or failed commands |

`stator` is also a sample-stream source in millimetres, so every capture
records the gap it was taken at. It reads `NaN` until the axis is homed, which
is deliberate: a capture with a `NaN` stator column is one whose position you
should not trust.

Configuration is not persisted. `rig_stator_datum` in particular must be set
after every reflash, or its compile-time default in `src/config.rs` updated.
This follows the same convention as `rig_laser_range`.

### Homing

Homing is the only way the axis learns where it is. The sequence:

1. Energise the driver and wait for it to wake.
2. If the opto sensor says the stage is on the advanced side of the edge,
   retract at the fast rate until it crosses back.
3. Retract a further `STATOR_HOME_BACKOFF_MM` to clear the edge and any lash.
4. Advance at the slow rate until the edge is crossed. That point is the datum,
   and the step counter is zeroed there.

The final motion is always a slow advance, which is what makes the datum
repeatable and leaves the mechanism in the standard contact state. Every search
is bounded by `STATOR_SEEK_MAX_MM`; exceeding it faults rather than continuing,
since the alternative is driving into a hard stop.

The opto sensor is an **edge**, not a vane, so its state alone says which side
of the datum the stage is on and the search cannot get lost past a narrow flag.

### Moves and the unidirectional approach

Every move ends with an advance:

- Target ahead of the current position: advance straight to it.
- Target behind the current position: retract past it by `rig_stator_backlash`,
  then advance onto it.
- Position not settled, because a previous move was aborted mid-retract: the
  retract-then-advance sequence is forced regardless of direction.

### Suggested working protocol

Move, settle, capture. Do not step during a capture. The firmware does not
enforce this, because a hard interlock would get in the way of the
characterisation described below, but the `stator` source makes any violation
visible after the fact: a capture whose `stator` column is constant is
quiescent by inspection.

## Commissioning and calibration

Do these in order, once, at commissioning, and record the results in
`notes.md`.

**1. Confirm the microstepping strapping by inspection**, and set
`STATOR_MICROSTEPS` to match. Do this before anything moves.

**2. Steps per millimetre.** The pitch is known exactly, so what is really
being verified is the steps-per-revolution assumption, the absence of gearing,
and the absence of lost steps. Command exactly 4000 full steps, nominally
12.7 mm, and read the barrel. Over that distance a ±5 µm manual reading is
0.04%, so any discrepancy means lost steps or a wrong constant, not calibration
error. Short moves cannot make this distinction, which is why the check is a
long one. A factor of eight is the failure to look for first.

**3. Datum offset.** Home, then read the micrometer barrel manually and write
that reading to `rig_stator_datum`. Update the compile-time default in
`src/config.rs` to match, so it survives a reflash.

**4. Datum repeatability.** Home ten times and record the spread in
`stator_steps`. Expect around 64 microsteps, matching the 1/1000 inch figure
from previous testing. A much larger spread means the slow approach rate is too
high or the backoff is too small.

**5. Backlash.** Approach the opto edge from both directions and difference the
step counts. That gives lash plus the sensor's own hysteresis. Repeat the
same-direction approach to get hysteresis alone, and subtract. Set
`rig_stator_backlash` comfortably above the result, and update the default in
`src/config.rs`. Without a preload spring, expect this to be large.

**6. Travel limits.** With the datum established, find the usable travel by
hand, and set `STATOR_TRAVEL_BELOW_DATUM_MM` and `STATOR_TRAVEL_ABOVE_DATUM_MM`
to a conservative subset of it. They default to ±5 mm about the datum, which is
a guess made before the geometry was known and should be replaced by a measured
figure.

**7. Lost-step audit.** At the end of each session, re-home and read
`stator_home_error`. It has a noise floor of about 64 microsteps, so treat
anything within that as agreement and anything well outside it as evidence that
steps were lost during the session.

## Noise characterisation

The rig measures a sense coil at a few hundred microvolts and takes laser
distance over RS422. A chopper-mode stepper driver switching amps at around
100 kHz, on a permanent-magnet motor, next to both of them, is a plausible
source of contamination through at least three routes: conducted coupling
through shared grounds, radiated coupling into the coil, and direct magnetic
coupling from the rotor.

There is an existing baseline to compare against. The disarmed capture of
2026-08-12, recorded in `notes.md`, gave `coil` spanning −0.61 to +0.23 mV with
a standard deviation of 0.12 mV, at the converter's quantisation floor.

Repeat that same disarmed capture in four conditions and compare directly:

1. motor unpowered, as the control;
2. motor supply on, driver de-energised via ENABLE;
3. driver energised and holding, not stepping;
4. stepping at the working rate.

Condition 3 is the one that matters most for routine use, because without
ENABLE it is the resting state of the rig. Condition 4 tells you whether the
move-settle-capture protocol is a convenience or a requirement.

Separately, re-run `helic-rt-regression` with the stepper moving, to confirm
that `loop_time_max`, tick rate, and dropped records are undisturbed. The
architecture says they must be, since nothing on the axis touches core 1, but
the claim should be measured rather than asserted.

## Safety and interlocks

- **The axis refuses to move while the output safety gate is armed**, and
  aborts an in-progress move if the gate becomes armed. Moving the stator while
  the exciter is driving changes the specimen's rest displacement underneath a
  running controller.
- **Interaction with the displacement window.** `DISPLACEMENT_MIN_MM` and
  `DISPLACEMENT_MAX_MM` in `src/config.rs` are a fixed 10–40 mm window about the
  approximately 25 mm rest point, and the gate latches a fault outside it.
  Moving the stator moves the specimen's rest displacement, so a stator travel
  wide enough to push the rest point outside that window will trip the gate on
  a perfectly healthy rig. Re-check these two constants against the intended
  stator travel before commissioning. Widening the window weakens the guard; the
  alternative is to make it relative to a rest point that tracks stator
  position, which is a firmware change and not currently implemented.
- **Position does not survive a reboot.** The step counter is RAM state, so the
  axis comes up as not homed and refuses absolute moves until homed.
- **A reboot request stops the axis** and de-energises the driver.
- Soft travel limits are enforced before motion, and out-of-range targets are
  rejected rather than clamped, so a mistyped target fails visibly.

## Limitations

- Open loop. The opto sensor is the only position feedback and it reports one
  point, not a continuous measurement. Lost steps are invisible until the next
  home.
- No acceleration ramp. The traverse rate defaults to 0.5 mm/s, roughly
  157 full steps per second, comfortably below the pull-in rate of a small
  stepper under load. Raising it much will need a ramp, which the PIO design
  accommodates but the firmware does not currently implement.
- Aborting a move leaves up to eight microsteps queued in the PIO FIFO. The
  firmware drains the FIFO before reporting a final position, so the count stays
  exact, but the stage travels those few microns after the abort is issued.
- The step counter leads reality by up to the FIFO depth during a move, since
  it counts words queued rather than pulses emitted. This matters only for
  telemetry read mid-move, and is under 4 µm.

## Where this code should eventually live

`src/step_pio.rs` is a portable PIO step generator with nothing rig-specific in
it. By the rule in `AGENTS.md` it belongs in the platform, in `helic-fw-rt`,
alongside the existing `ssi_pio` and `pulse_pio` helpers. It is here because
this rig is its only consumer today and the platform is pinned to a released
tag. Lift it upstream when a second rig needs it, and keep the boundary between
it and `src/stator.rs`, which is entirely rig-specific, clean enough that the
lift stays cheap.
